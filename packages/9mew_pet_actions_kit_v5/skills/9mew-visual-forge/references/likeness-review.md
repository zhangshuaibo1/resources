# Naimiao image-quality review

## Identity authority

Use `assets/naimiao-master.png` as the sole identity authority for the current main cat; verify its hash in `assets/identity.json`. The rejected GLB and its derived images/animations are excluded from current product use and positive references. Current work uses 2D art and planar animation. Keep 3D deferred.

## Current checkpoints

1. **Original identity and actual image.** Compare face width, cheek fullness, head tilt, eye placement and eyelids, short muzzle, ear flare, compact body, toes and fluffy tail. Preserve natural expression and fine fur edges. Review at full resolution and intended website size. Prefer the unchanged original to an inferior redraw.
2. **Composition and quality.** Inspect on warm ivory and midnight surfaces. Check alpha edges, coat/eye detail, contrast, pose, prop contact, margins and real UI safe areas. Keep effects clear of the eyes and face. Do not flatten the cat into simpler geometry or force extra assets at the cost of quality.
3. **Required pet performance.** Watch each actual action at its intended timing and inspect all source frames. For real limb/expression animation, compare identity, limb count, paw contact, torso/tail relationship, fur and lighting throughout the sequence. A jump needs crouch, takeoff, airborne tuck and landing; locomotion needs alternating feet; emotions need facial/posture changes. Check pauses, reduced motion and hidden/offscreen states. Correct failed motion and keep it pending while the runtime uses the selected still. Whole-image motion-posters remain decorative.

These checkpoints use existing task authorization and the owning project's activation process. They do not add mandatory confirmation rounds for ordinary reversible work. The current pet-action scope requires actual verified character animation. A high-quality static fallback keeps the site usable but does not satisfy a missing action.

## Defects to correct

- A broad round face becoming long, pinched or asymmetrical.
- Embedded almond-rounded eyes becoming beads, discs or duplicated eyelids.
- A natural muzzle flattened or replaced by a drawn line smile.
- Ear, paw or tail outlines losing their identity, fur detail or elegant shape.
- Faces, limbs or props drifting, stretching, sliding or changing count between frames.
- Chopped alpha edges, halos, holes from separated layers or accessories masking defects.
- Overactive bobbing, large rotations, excessive glitter or effects covering the character.
- Calling a whole-image translation a blink, wave, articulated breath or real 3D movement.

## Evidence and future upgrade

Keep the original reference hash, actual image/frame/source paths and concrete findings. Do not invent a similarity score. Distinguish a still, keyframe guide, motion-poster and real character animation.

A future 3D scope may add same-camera mesh-form, material/fur and runtime checks after a capable authorized workflow exists. No mesh or GLB is required in the current 2D completion definition; an inferred angle sheet does not substitute for future geometry.
